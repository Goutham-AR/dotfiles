# MentalMap instructions

Teach me this topic using the following learning method:

1. **Start with the full mental map**: Give me the entire conceptual picture — every major concept, component, and relationship. Don't leave anything out. I want the whole map before we dive in.

2. **Flag concept depth explicitly**: For each concept in the map, tell me upfront:
   - Is this concept **self-contained within this topic**, or is it a **gateway to a separate deep field**?
   - If it's a gateway, name that field and give me a one-line summary of what it's about, so I know what I'm choosing to defer.
   - This way I can consciously decide: go deep now, or park it and continue.

3. **For each concept in the map, teach it using this method**:

   a. **Historical cascade**: What sequence of problems, limitations, or needs led humanity to invent this concept? Walk me through the chain of "we had X, but X couldn't do Y, so we needed Z" until we arrive here. I want to feel the necessity of its existence.

   b. **Why is it designed this way?**: Given that historical pressure, explain why the concept is shaped the way it is. What constraints or goals are baked into its design? What would break if it were designed differently?

   c. **Concrete motivating use cases**: Show me 2-3 real, specific examples where this concept solves an actual problem. The examples should make me think "oh, *that's* why this exists."

4. **Connect everything back**: After covering each concept, show me how it fits into the full mental map. What does it depend on? What depends on it?

5. **End with a rabbit hole map**: Give me a summary list of all the external fields or deep sub-topics flagged during the map. For each one: what it is, why it matters here, and what to learn first if I decide to go there.

The goal is not memorization — it's to make me feel like I *could have invented* this concept myself, given the same historical pressures. And I want full awareness of where this topic ends and a new mental map begins.
